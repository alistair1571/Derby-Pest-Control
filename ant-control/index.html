Ants are the most common insect call out we get outside of wasp season, usually turning up on kitchen worktops or along skirting boards in spring and summer. Most cases in Derby are one of two species, occasionally a third, and knowing which one makes a real difference to how quickly it's actually sorted.

This page covers how to tell the species apart, what Flying Ant Day actually is, what to look for, and what a professional visit involves. If you just want it sorted, call the number above, we'll confirm a fixed price on the phone before anything is booked.

Why Ants Are a Problem

Most ant activity in Derby is the common black garden ant (Lasius niger), which nests outdoors and only comes inside to forage for food. Once the outdoor nest is treated, the indoor trail clears with it, and most jobs are straightforward. They leave a pheromone trail as they travel, which is why a couple of ants spotted in the morning can be a steady stream by evening, and why the trail so often reappears in a slightly different spot after being wiped away, the scent, and the nest sending them out, are both still there.

Pharaoh ants are a different problem entirely. Pale, tiny, and unable to survive outdoors through a UK winter, they're found exclusively in consistently heated buildings, commonly hospitals, care homes, and older properties that have been subdivided into flats or HMOs. A single pharaoh ant colony can hold anywhere up to hundreds of thousands of ants nesting deep in wall cavities and behind fittings, which makes them considerably harder to clear and more likely to spread between neighbouring units if not treated properly.

Getting the Species Right

There's a third species worth knowing about too, since it's the one most likely to catch someone out. The common red ant (Myrmica rubra) is smaller and reddish brown, nests under paving slabs, logs and stones in the garden, and unlike black garden ants, genuinely stings rather than just bites, a surprisingly painful one if disturbed. If you've been stung rather than just irritated by ants crawling across skin, it's more likely this species than the common black garden ant, and it's worth mentioning when you call so we know what we're dealing with before we arrive.

To locate an outdoor nest, look for small piles of fine soil or wood shavings near skirting boards, patios or paving, a telltale sign of the entrance rather than just where ants happen to be foraging.

Flying Ant Day and Seasonal Activity

If you've ever stepped outside on a warm, humid day in July or August to find the air suddenly full of flying ants, that's not an infestation getting worse, it's a normal part of the black garden ant's yearly cycle. Colonies produce winged males and new queens that swarm together to mate, after which the new queens disperse to start colonies of their own elsewhere. It tends to happen on the same kind of muggy, still afternoon across a wide area at once, which is why it can feel like it's happening everywhere simultaneously.

It usually settles on its own within a day or two and doesn't need treating differently to a normal ant problem. What's actually worth calling about is if the underlying trail or nest activity in your kitchen or garden hasn't eased off once the swarm has passed.

What Damage Can Ants Do?

Ants don't cause structural damage the way rodents or wood boring insects can, but they're still more than a nuisance. In a kitchen, they contaminate food and worktops as they forage, and in a commercial setting an ant trail across a food prep area can be enough to fail a hygiene inspection. Pharaoh ants specifically are a recognised concern in healthcare settings, since they're capable of spreading bacteria between wards or rooms as they travel through wall cavities and behind fittings.

Signs You Have an Ant Problem

  • Ant trails along skirting boards or kitchen worktops
  • Ants appearing repeatedly in the same room despite cleaning
  • Small piles of fine soil or wood shavings near skirting boards, patios or paving, a sign of a nest entrance close by
  • Visible nest activity in garden beds, patios, or under decking
  • More ants indoors after rain, as outdoor nests move to drier ground
  • A genuine sting rather than a bite, worth mentioning when you call, since it points to red ants rather than black garden ants

How Do You Get Rid of Ants?

Wiping away a visible trail only ever buys a short amount of time, since the ants that were following it will usually find a new route within a day or two. The trail is a symptom, not the source. Shop bought powders can also put non-target insects and garden wildlife at risk if spread widely rather than applied precisely to a confirmed nest.

The more effective approach treats the nest itself, which for most Derby properties is outdoors, under patios, decking, or in flower beds, even when the only sign noticed has been indoors. For pharaoh ants nesting inside a building, treatment needs to be far more targeted, since the usual outdoor nest treatment won't reach them at all.

How We Effectively Remove Ants

We identify the species and, where possible, the nest source before treating, rather than just spraying where ants have been seen. For black garden ants, that usually means treatment both indoors and at the outdoor nest. For pharaoh ants and red ants, the approach is genuinely different, and getting the identification right on the first visit is what actually stops a job needing repeat call outs.

Products and application methods are chosen to be appropriate for kitchens and food preparation areas, and we'll talk you through anything that needs a surface wipe down before use. If the visible trail keeps returning after treatment, it's often a sign the nest wasn't fully reached the first time, and a second visit is sometimes needed for persistent gardens at no extra charge for that follow-up.

Common questions

Why do the ants keep coming back?

If only the visible trail is treated and not the nest, ants will often find a new route in. We aim to treat the source, but for persistent gardens a second visit is sometimes needed.

Is treatment safe near food preparation areas?

Yes, we use products and application methods appropriate for kitchens, and we'll talk you through anything that needs a surface wipe down before use.

Do you treat gardens as well as inside the house?

Yes, most ant nests are actually outdoors, under patios, decking or in flower beds, so outdoor treatment is often part of the job.

What is Flying Ant Day and do I need to worry about it?

It's not one single day everywhere, but a real seasonal pattern, black garden ants produce winged males and new queens that swarm on warm, humid days in July and August to mate and start new colonies elsewhere. It's a sign of normal breeding behaviour, not a bigger infestation, and it usually settles within a day or two on its own.

I got stung, not just bitten, is that normal for ants?

Black garden ants don't sting, so if you've been genuinely stung rather than bitten, it's more likely the common red ant, a smaller, reddish species that nests under paving slabs, logs and stones and delivers a surprisingly painful sting when disturbed. Let us know if that's what you're dealing with, it needs a different approach.

Why do pharaoh ants keep turning up in my building specifically?

Pharaoh ants can't survive UK winters outdoors, so they're only ever found in consistently heated buildings, commonly hospitals, care homes, and older properties that have been subdivided into flats or HMOs. If that sounds like your building, it's worth getting the species confirmed early, since pharaoh ants spread between units through shared wall cavities and need a different treatment to garden ants.
