Bed bugs don't reflect on how clean a home is. They travel in on luggage, secondhand furniture, or clothing, and can turn up anywhere from a family home to a hotel room. What matters is catching it early, since numbers build quickly once they're established, and cases in Derby do tend to rise after the summer holiday season and again after Christmas, both times when more people are travelling and bringing luggage home.

This page covers why a shop bought spray so often doesn't finish the job, the difference between heat and spray treatment, and what to expect from a professional visit. If you just want it sorted, call the number above, we'll confirm a fixed price on the phone before anything is booked.

Why Bed Bugs Are a Problem

Bed bug bites cause itching and, for some people, a more significant allergic reaction, and the psychological toll of an infestation, disrupted sleep, anxiety about the bedroom, can be just as difficult to deal with as the physical bites themselves.

In flats, HMOs and shared buildings, an untreated infestation in one unit can spread to neighbouring properties through wall cavities and shared pipework, which is why landlords often need adjoining rooms checked as part of the job, not just the one that's been reported.

Why a Shop Bought Spray Often Doesn't Finish the Job

It's one of the most common things we hear, a spray from the shop was tried first and it didn't work. That's rarely down to how it was used. Bed bug populations in many parts of the UK have developed genuine resistance to several of the insecticides commonly sold for home use, so a product that worked well years ago can now have little real effect on a modern, resistant population. Professional treatment uses products chosen for what actually still works, rotated and applied based on what's known to be effective, rather than whatever happens to be on a shop shelf.

DIY attempts also tend to miss where bed bugs actually hide. Their flat profile lets them live in the tiniest cracks and crevices, mattress seams, bed frame joints, skirting boards, and behind headboards, and a spray applied only to the visible mattress surface usually misses most of the population entirely.

What Damage Can Bed Bugs Cause?

Bed bugs don't damage a property structurally, but a heavy infestation can mean replacing mattresses, bedding and soft furnishings that are too badly affected to treat, on top of the cost of the treatment itself.

For landlords and letting agents, an unresolved infestation can mean a property standing empty for longer between tenancies while it's properly cleared, since bed bugs are notoriously difficult to eliminate without professional treatment.

Signs You Have Bed Bugs

  • Small reddish-brown insects, about the size of an apple seed, in mattress seams or bed frame joints
  • Tiny dark spotting on sheets or mattress edges (droppings)
  • Clusters of itchy bites, often in a line, on waking
  • A faint musty odour in the affected room
  • Tiny pale eggs or shed skins in mattress seams, bed frame joints, or behind headboards

Treatment Options: Spray, Steam or Heat?

There isn't one single correct method, it depends on how established the infestation is. Targeted spray treatment, sometimes combined with steam applied directly into seams and joints, suits lighter or more contained problems and is often the quickest to arrange. Heat treatment raises a room to a temperature that kills bed bugs at every life stage, including eggs, in a single session without chemicals, and tends to suit heavier or more established infestations, or situations where a fast, thorough result matters most.

We'll recommend whichever genuinely fits your situation after the inspection, rather than defaulting to one method regardless of what's actually needed.

We begin with a full inspection to confirm the presence of bed bugs and the extent of the infestation, checking the bed, frame, furniture, skirting boards and behind headboards so no area is overlooked. Treatment then covers the whole affected room, not just the mattress.

Visits are discreet, with nothing that flags the issue to neighbours, and we'll talk you through simple preparation beforehand, such as washing bedding on a hot cycle, that makes the treatment more effective. Most single rooms need one visit plus the included follow-up. Heavier infestations across multiple rooms may need a more thorough full property treatment, which we'd price separately after the first inspection. Common questions

Do I need to throw away my mattress?

Usually not. Treatment is designed to clear an infestation without needing to replace the mattress, though badly affected items are sometimes best discarded. We'll advise on the visit.

How many treatments will I need?

Most bedrooms need one treatment plus the included follow-up. More established infestations across multiple rooms may need an additional visit, which we'd discuss and price upfront.

Is the treatment safe with children or pets in the house?

Yes, though we'll give clear guidance on re-entry times for the treated room and how to prepare it beforehand.

I've already tried a spray from the shop and it didn't work, why?

This is really common, and it's not usually about how it was applied. Bed bug populations in many parts of the UK have developed genuine resistance to some of the insecticides sold for home use, so a product that worked well a decade ago can now have little effect. Professional treatment uses products and methods chosen for what actually works against resistant populations, not just what's available on a shelf.

Is heat treatment better than spray treatment?

Neither is universally better, they suit different situations. Heat treatment raises a room to a temperature that kills bed bugs at every life stage, including eggs, in one session, without chemicals, and works well for heavier or more established infestations. Spray and targeted treatment, sometimes combined with steam applied directly to seams and joints, suits lighter or more contained problems and is often quicker to arrange. We'll recommend whichever actually fits your situation rather than defaulting to one option.

Is there a time of year this is more common?

Bed bugs are active indoors year-round, but cases do rise after the summer holiday season and again after Christmas, both periods when more people travel and bring luggage into the home. If you've just been away, it's worth checking a little sooner rather than later.
