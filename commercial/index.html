The Legal Risk of Getting This Wrong

Food businesses in England are required under the Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013 and the Food Safety Act 1990 to prevent contamination from pests, and pest control specifically falls under Regulation 19 of the 2013 regulations. This isn't a formality: local authority environmental health officers actively enforce it, and the penalties are real. A magistrates' court can fine a business up to £20,000 per offence, and cases serious enough to reach Crown Court can carry unlimited fines and up to two years in prison. In 2025 a national retailer was fined over £250,000 in a single case after repeated pest control failures were found during routine inspections.

There's a reputational cost too. Pest activity found during an inspection can directly affect your Food Hygiene Rating, published publicly on the Food Standards Agency website for anyone, including your customers, to see. A scheduled contract with documented visits protects against both the legal risk and the public rating at the same time, which is why it's treated as a standing cost of doing business rather than something to deal with reactively.

What's Included in a Commercial Contract

Every commercial contract includes BPCA-standard treatment carried out by a technician who knows what an inspection actually requires, not just a general pest visit. That includes ULV (Ultra Low Volume) fogging where flying insects are the issue, a fine insecticide vapour that clears an infestation fast without soaking the premises. A written report is left after every call, covering what was checked, what was found and any action taken, along with COSHH information and risk assessments where the premises needs them.

Visit frequency is agreed at the site survey rather than sold as a fixed package. Food businesses and commercial kitchens typically need monthly visits given the regulatory exposure, while lower-risk premises such as offices or retail units often manage well on a quarterly schedule. For warehouses and large industrial units specifically, we also look at bird risk as standard, pigeons and gulls are drawn to big open doors and high roof spaces for safe nesting, and beyond the mess, nests and feathers are a genuine electrical fire hazard while droppings can contaminate stock. Contracts run for an agreed term with no automatic lock-in beyond it, and visits are scheduled around your operating hours rather than disrupting trade.

Common questions

What happens if my business fails a pest check?

An environmental health officer can order a food business to stop trading on the spot if pest activity poses an immediate risk, and can prosecute afterwards. Magistrates' courts can fine up to £20,000 per offence under food safety law, and cases sent to Crown Court can carry unlimited fines and up to two years in prison. A national retailer was fined over £250,000 in a single case in 2025 for repeated pest control failures.

How often do I need commercial pest control visits?

It depends on the premises and risk level. Food businesses and commercial kitchens typically need monthly visits, retail and office premises often manage well on a quarterly schedule. We'll recommend a frequency at the site survey rather than a one-size answer.

Will I get documentation I can show an inspector?

Yes, a written report is left after every visit covering what was checked, what was found and any action taken, along with COSHH information and risk assessments where relevant. It's built to be produced at inspection without notice, not requested and chased up afterwards.

Do you cover food hygiene rating concerns as well as legal risk?

Yes. Pest activity can affect your Food Hygiene Rating as well as your legal exposure, and that rating is public on the Food Standards Agency website. Scheduled visits protect both at once.

Can you work around our trading hours?

Yes, visits are scheduled at a time that suits your operation, before opening, after close, or during a quiet period, rather than disrupting service.

Do you deal with birds as well as rats and insects in commercial premises?

Yes, this comes up a lot with warehouses and large industrial units specifically. Big open doors and high roof spaces make ideal nesting conditions for pigeons and gulls, and beyond the obvious mess, nests and feathers are a genuine electrical fire hazard, with droppings capable of contaminating stock. We factor bird risk into the survey for larger units as standard.
