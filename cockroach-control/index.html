Why Cockroaches Are a Problem

Cockroaches pick up bacteria such as salmonella and E. coli on their legs and bodies as they move through drains and waste, then transfer it onto food surfaces, worktops and utensils. The health risk goes further than droppings, though: cockroaches shed their exoskeleton repeatedly as they grow, and those shed skins break down into a fine dust that spreads through a kitchen, a recognised trigger for asthma and allergies, particularly in children. They also regurgitate partly digested food while feeding, leaving behind saliva and digestive residue whichever surfaces they've been across.

They breed extremely quickly, too. A single female German cockroach can produce well over 300 offspring across her lifetime, carried in purse-shaped egg cases called oothecae that she keeps with her until they're close to hatching, which is exactly why commercial kitchens treat an infestation as urgent rather than cosmetic.

German or Oriental? Getting the Species Right

The German cockroach is what we treat in almost every Derby case, small, light brown to tan, with two dark stripes running down behind the head, and a strong preference for warm, humid spots like behind fridges, boiler cupboards and under sinks. Occasionally, usually in cooler, damper areas like basements, drains, ground floor utility rooms or older cellars, we'll find the Oriental cockroach instead, noticeably larger, darker, almost black, and less tolerant of the dry warmth a German cockroach thrives in.

The two need a genuinely different approach, since they favour different harbourage spots and respond differently to bait placement, which is why we confirm the species on the visit rather than treat on assumption. Cockroaches are also remarkably hardy, able to survive without water for around two weeks, which is part of why good hygiene alone reduces a problem without ever fully clearing an established one.

What Damage Can Cockroaches Cause?

Cockroaches don't damage the structure of a building, but the practical cost of an infestation can still be significant. Contaminated stock, closed sections of a commercial kitchen, and reputational damage from a sighting during service can far outweigh the cost of treatment, and under the Food Safety Act, a food business found with an active infestation is exposed to real enforcement risk, not just embarrassment.

In a home, a heavy infestation can mean throwing out contaminated food packaging and a deep clean of affected cupboards and appliances on top of the treatment itself.

Signs You Have a Cockroach Problem

  • Small light brown cockroaches, roughly 13 to 16mm long, with two dark stripes behind the head, the German cockroach, by far the most common indoor species in the UK
  • A larger, darker, almost black cockroach in a basement, drain or ground floor utility room, more likely the Oriental cockroach
  • Live cockroaches seen at night in kitchens or bathrooms
  • Seeing them during the day, often a sign of a larger population
  • A musty, oily smell in cupboards
  • Small dark droppings resembling ground pepper
  • Oval brown egg cases (oothecae) behind appliances or in cracks

How Do You Get Rid of Cockroaches?

Cockroaches are more resilient than most household pests, and a spray-only approach can make things worse by scattering a colony into other rooms rather than clearing it. Shop bought sprays kill what they touch but rarely reach the areas where cockroaches are actually breeding, and because a female can survive on very little for weeks, a problem that looks resolved after a spray often just resurfaces once egg cases hatch.

Treatment needs to target the nest, usually behind fridges, under sinks, or inside appliance housings, not just the cockroaches you can see. This is why professional treatment using gel bait tends to work far better than surface sprays: it relies on a secondary kill effect, where cockroaches that feed on it carry it back to the colony, and others that come into contact with the affected insect or its droppings are cleared too, without needing to individually treat every cockroach in the harbourage.

How We Effectively Remove Cockroaches

We start with a full property or kitchen survey to identify the species and harbourage points, then apply targeted gel bait and residual treatment rather than a scattering spray. This lets cockroaches carry bait back to the colony, treating the source rather than just what's visible, and results typically build over one to two weeks as the secondary kill effect works through the population.

We'll also advise on reducing food and water sources that are drawing them in. A follow-up visit is included to confirm the treatment has worked, since egg cases can hatch after the first visit and a second check catches anything that's emerged since. Visits are kept discreet, unmarked vehicles and no fuss, which matters as much for a domestic kitchen as it does for a restaurant mid-service.

In flats or HMOs, a colony can move between neighbouring properties through shared pipework, which is why commercial and landlord clients often need adjoining units checked too. If we genuinely don't have access to a source property causing repeat activity, we'll say so honestly rather than promise a result we can't guarantee. Common questions

Why do I keep seeing them even after cleaning?

Cleaning removes food sources but doesn't clear an established harbourage. Treatment targets the nest itself, not just visible activity.

Is this covered under your commercial contracts?

Yes, cockroach control is one of the most common reasons commercial clients set up a scheduled contract.

How many visits will I need?

Most domestic jobs clear within two visits, spaced two to three weeks apart to catch newly hatched egg cases.

How does gel bait actually work?

It relies on what's called a secondary kill effect. A cockroach that feeds on the gel carries it back to the harbourage and dies there, and other cockroaches that come into contact with the corpse or its droppings are affected too. That's how one bait point can clear far more of a colony than the cockroaches that fed on it directly, which is also why results build over a couple of weeks rather than overnight.

Is it definitely a German cockroach, or could it be something else?

Almost everything we see in Derby homes and kitchens is the German cockroach, small, light brown, two dark stripes. Occasionally, usually in cooler, damper spots like basements, drains or ground floor utility rooms, we'll find the Oriental cockroach instead, larger, darker, almost black. The two need slightly different treatment, which is why we confirm the species on the visit rather than assume.

What if the cockroaches are coming from a neighbouring property?

It happens, particularly in flats, HMOs and shared buildings, and it's worth being upfront about: if we don't have access to the source property, full eradication can be genuinely difficult until that's addressed too. We'll always tell you honestly if that's what's going on rather than promise something we can't deliver.
