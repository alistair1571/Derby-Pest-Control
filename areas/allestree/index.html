About Allestree

Allestree Park is the largest open space in Derby at around 320 acres, mature woodland, open grassland, a 7-acre lake and, at its centre, Allestree Hall, a Grade II* listed former country house built in 1795 by the architect James Wyatt. The hall has stood empty for years and sits on the Heritage at Risk Register, but the parkland around it has been a Local Nature Reserve since 2002, and its woodland is genuinely old, full of veteran trees and the kind of standing deadwood that takes decades to develop.

Large parts of the surrounding estate were sold for housing in 1928, which is why Allestree today is a mix of interwar semis, later detached housing and mature gardens close to the park itself. The golf course that occupied much of the parkland for decades closed in December 2020, and much of that land has since reverted to more open, less manicured parkland. Most of Allestree is also a designated conservation area, one of 15 across Derby, which can mean needing planning consent for certain visible changes to a property's exterior.

Why Allestree Sees More Squirrel Activity

Most of our Derby area pages talk about wasps and rodents as the main local pattern. Allestree is a genuine exception, and it comes down to the park. Old, undisturbed woodland with plenty of mature trees and natural deadwood, exactly what Allestree Park has in abundance, is prime squirrel habitat, and a garden backing directly onto that kind of woodland gives squirrels a short, easy route from a tree cavity into a roof space.

That matters because squirrels aren't treated the same way as rats or mice. Rodenticide bait isn't licensed or effective on them, and because trapped grey squirrels can't legally be released under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981, the job needs humane trapping carried out properly, not a generic rodent visit. If you're in a street backing onto the park specifically, it's worth mentioning that when you call, since it changes what we expect to find. Our squirrel control page covers the full legal and practical picture.

Weather, Seasons and How They Affect Pests in Allestree

Like the rest of Derby, Allestree sits in a mild, wet, temperate climate, with summers that are warm without being extreme, usually peaking in the high teens to low twenties Celsius through July and August.

That kind of summer is exactly what extends the wasp and insect season in areas like Allestree. Warmth and regular rain keep gardens, sheds and the parkland's hedgerows growing right through the season, giving wasps and ants more time and more undisturbed spots to settle into than a shorter, harsher summer would allow. Squirrel activity follows a different pattern, picking up in autumn as they cache food for winter and again in late winter and early spring during breeding season, when a loft space becomes an attractive place to nest.

How to Reduce Pest Problems in Allestree

A few practical, area-specific steps that genuinely help, on top of anything a professional treatment covers.

  • If your garden backs onto the park or any mature woodland nearby, check your roofline and eaves each spring for gaps a squirrel could exploit, before breeding season is in full swing.
  • Keep bird feeders tidy and off the ground where possible. Spilled seed draws squirrels as readily as it draws birds, especially close to the park boundary.
  • Check shed and garage doors for gaps each spring, before a wasp nest has the chance to establish for the season.
  • If proofing work involves visible changes to brickwork, airbricks or the roofline on a street-facing wall, check whether conservation area consent is needed before starting, since most of Allestree falls under that designation.

Common questions

How quickly can you get to a pest problem in Allestree?

Most Allestree addresses get a technician same day, and we prioritise active infestations and wasp nests within 2 to 4 hours.

What pests are most common in Allestree specifically?

Wasps, garden ants and squirrels lead the way, largely down to how much mature woodland and parkland border residential gardens here. We still treat the full range, rats, mice, insects and more, but these three are what we're called out for most.

Why does a park nearby mean more squirrels, not just more wasps?

Allestree Park's woodland is old and largely undisturbed, plenty of mature trees with natural cavities and deadwood, which is exactly the kind of habitat squirrels favour for nesting. A garden backing onto that kind of woodland gives squirrels a short, easy route into a loft, which is why we see more squirrel call outs here than in most other parts of Derby.

I'm in a conservation area, does that change anything?

Most of Allestree is a designated conservation area, which can mean needing consent for certain visible external changes to a property. It doesn't affect treatment itself, but if proofing work involves altering brickwork, airbricks or roofline details on the street-facing side, it's worth checking with the council first. We'll flag if that's likely to apply during a survey.

Do you cover the whole of Allestree, not just near the park?

Yes, all of it, including streets further from the park where the pattern is closer to Derby's wider suburban mix. See our areas covered page for everywhere else we reach.
