Your Local Pest Control Expert in Oakwood

Oakwood is one of the newer parts of Derby we cover, and newer housing genuinely does mean a different set of problems, which is why we don't treat it the same way as Derby's Victorian suburbs. There's a genuine exception to that pattern here, though, and it's the reason Oakwood has its name in the first place.

About Oakwood

Oakwood is built on land that was once known as Chaddesden Common, acquired for development in the 1980s despite local opposition at the time, with construction and expansion continuing through the 1990s and beyond. It's a largely modern estate, with tighter cavity wall construction than the city's older housing stock, and new streets have continued to be added as the estate has grown.

The name comes from something genuinely worth knowing about: the original oak wood the area was built around has survived intact as Chaddesden Wood Nature Reserve, declared a Local Nature Reserve in 1991, and it's the only ancient oak woodland remaining anywhere in the city of Derby. A nature trail runs through it, and in spring it's covered in bluebells, popular with walkers from across the north of the city, not just Oakwood itself.

Why an Ancient Wood Changes the Picture Here

That construction standard makes a real difference to rodents. Entry points for rats and mice are less common here than in Derby's Victorian and interwar suburbs, simply because there are fewer gaps for them to exploit in modern cavity wall housing. What we see far more of instead is wasps and ants, drawn to the newer gardens, sheds and eaves that come with a younger housing estate, and that's the dominant pattern across most of Oakwood.

Squirrels are the genuine exception, and it comes down to Chaddesden Wood specifically. Ancient woodland with mature trees and natural cavities is exactly the habitat squirrels favour for nesting, and unlike a typical newer estate with no significant woodland nearby, Oakwood has the real thing on its doorstep. A property backing onto or close to the wood sees more squirrel activity than its age and construction standard would otherwise predict, and squirrels don't need the same kind of gap a rat does to get into a loft, so tight modern construction doesn't rule them out the way it does for rodents. If that's what you're dealing with, our squirrel control page covers the legal and practical picture, since they're treated very differently to rats and mice.

Weather, Seasons and How They Affect Pests in Oakwood

Like the rest of Derby, Oakwood sits in a mild, wet, temperate climate, with summers that are warm without being extreme, usually peaking in the high teens to low twenties Celsius through July and August.

That kind of summer is exactly what extends the wasp and insect season here. Warmth and regular rain keep gardens, sheds and hedgerows growing right through the season, giving wasps and ants more time and more undisturbed spots to settle into. Squirrel activity follows a different pattern, picking up in autumn as they cache food for winter and again in late winter and early spring during breeding season, when a nearby loft becomes an attractive place to nest.

How to Reduce Pest Problems in Oakwood

A few practical, area-specific steps that genuinely help, on top of anything a professional treatment covers.

  • If your garden backs onto or sits near Chaddesden Wood, check your roofline and eaves each spring for gaps a squirrel could exploit, before breeding season is in full swing.
  • Check shed and garage doors for gaps each spring, before a wasp nest has the chance to establish for the season.
  • Keep bird feeders tidy and off the ground where possible, especially near the wood, since spilled seed draws squirrels as readily as it draws birds.
  • Keep compost bins and garden waste reasonably tidy, since undisturbed corners are exactly what ants and wasps look for when choosing where to nest.
