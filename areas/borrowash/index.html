About Borrowash

Borrowash grew from being the smaller of two villages in the ancient parish of Ockbrook into the larger of the two by the late 1800s, and today it's a busy commuter village between Derby and Nottingham, with the historic Derby Canal, now being restored, running through its centre.

The mix of older village housing near the canal and later estate development further out gives Borrowash a similar split to Derby's other historic villages: ageing brickwork near the centre for rodents to exploit, and more wasps and garden insects in the newer streets further from the water.

Weather, Seasons and How They Affect Pests in Borrowash

Like the rest of Derby, Borrowash sits in a mild, wet, temperate climate. Rain falls fairly evenly across the year rather than in a short wet season, and while individual downpours are rarely severe, the ground rarely gets the chance to dry out for long.

That matters more here than in most of Derby, because of Borrowash's position on the water. Rising water after heavy rain pushes rats out of drains, riverbanks and canal edges and toward the nearest dry ground, often a garden, shed or airbrick gap. Derby's mild winters also mean that unlike a harder UK frost, the cold here rarely forces rats into true dormancy, so this pattern isn't limited to summer storms; it can happen after any spell of sustained rain, at almost any time of year.

How to Reduce Pest Problems in Borrowash

A few practical, area-specific steps that genuinely help, on top of anything a professional treatment covers.

  • Given Borrowash's position near the water, secure bins with tight-fitting lids and clear any spilled bird seed or pet food promptly, especially after heavy rain, since rising water is exactly what pushes rats toward gardens looking for dry ground.
  • Check airbricks and ground-level gaps around the property after any spell of sustained wet weather, since these are the entry points rodents use most once they're displaced from riverbanks or drains nearby.
  • If you have a shed or outbuilding close to a garden boundary, keeping it clear of clutter removes one of the easiest places for a displaced rat to shelter undisturbed.
