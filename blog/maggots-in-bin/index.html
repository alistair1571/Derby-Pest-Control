How to Get Rid of Maggots in Your Bin

Maggots in a bin are unpleasant but almost always manageable yourself, and understanding why they appear in the first place makes it much easier to stop them coming back.

Why Maggots Appear

Maggots are fly larvae. Flies are drawn to exposed food waste, and once they land, they lay eggs directly on it. Those eggs hatch within 24 hours in warm weather, sometimes in as little as 8, which is why a bin can look completely fine one day and have a maggot problem the next. It's particularly common through spring and summer, when both fly activity and waste decomposition speed up in the heat.

Getting Rid of Them Now

Boiling water kills maggots instantly and is usually the fastest first step. Pour it directly over them, then follow up with a proper clean: a bleach or disinfectant solution (diluted according to the product's instructions), scrubbed into the corners and base of the bin with a long-handled brush, rinsed thoroughly, and left to dry fully before anything goes back in. Salt poured directly onto maggots also kills them by drawing out moisture, a useful option if boiling water isn't practical.

Stopping It Happening Again

The cycle repeats because flies keep finding a food source and somewhere to lay eggs, so removing both matters more than any one clean. Keep the bin lid firmly shut, since a lid that doesn't close properly, often from overfilling, is one of the most common causes. Double-bag food waste, particularly meat and fish, and freeze anything especially smelly until bin day if collections are infrequent. Rinsing food containers before binning them removes another food source flies are drawn to.

Common questions

Are maggots in a bin actually harmful?

Not directly to people, they're a hygiene nuisance rather than a health risk on their own, though the flies that follow can spread bacteria onto other surfaces.

Whose responsibility is it to clean the bin, mine or the council's?

Householders are generally responsible for cleaning their own bins, including wheelie bins provided by the council for collection.

What if this keeps happening even though I'm doing everything right?

For a one-off household bin, that's unusual. For a commercial premises with a bin store seeing repeat activity, it's worth treating as a wider pest and compliance issue rather than just a cleaning one.
