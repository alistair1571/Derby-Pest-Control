Are Honey Bees a Pest?

Short answer: no, not in the way a wasp nest is. Honey bees get called in about far more often than people expect, usually because they've been mistaken for wasps, and getting the identification right matters, since bees and wasps aren't treated the same way at all.

Why Bees Aren't Treated Like Wasps

Reputable pest controllers, following British Pest Control Association guidance, don't treat honey bees except where there's a genuine risk to health. Bee populations have been in serious decline for years, and bees are vital pollinators, so the default response to a bee sighting is identification and advice, not extermination.

Honey bees in particular are commonly mistaken for wasps by the public. They're rounder, hairier, and move more slowly and deliberately than the quicker, more angular flight of a wasp, worth knowing before assuming the worst about what's in your loft or shed.

What to Do About a Honey Bee Swarm

If you've got an established honey bee swarm, the usual advice is to contact the British Beekeepers Association for a local swarm collector, who will often collect it free of charge rather than have it exterminated. A swarm looks dramatic, a dense, moving cluster of bees, but it's a temporary stopover while scout bees look for a permanent nest site, not an attack.

Bumblebees and Solitary Bees

Bumblebee and solitary bee nests, often found in compost heaps, under sheds, or in holes in a lawn, are normally best left alone entirely. These colonies decline naturally by the end of July and won't return to the same spot the following year, so waiting them out is usually the simplest and most responsible option.

When It Genuinely Needs a Professional

There are limited situations where bee activity does need addressing, a nest built somewhere that creates a genuine, unavoidable risk to someone with a severe allergy, or a colony established inside a structure in a way that's causing real damage. Even then, the right response is usually relocation rather than extermination where it's achievable. If you're not sure what you're looking at or how urgent it is, that's completely normal, describe it to us and we'll help you work out what's actually going on before recommending anything.

Common questions

How do I tell a honey bee from a wasp?

Honey bees are rounder, hairier and duller in colour, and move more slowly and deliberately. Wasps are smoother, brighter yellow and black, and fly in a quicker, more darting pattern.

Will you remove a bee's nest if I ask?

We'll always help identify what you're dealing with first. If it's genuinely bees, we'll point you toward the right option, usually a beekeeper or swarm collector, rather than default to extermination.
