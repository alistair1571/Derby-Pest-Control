Why Wasp Nests Peak in August

August is consistently the busiest month for wasp call-outs, and it's not random, it's the natural end point of a colony's seasonal cycle that starts months earlier with a single queen.

How a Nest Starts

Every wasp nest begins with a single queen who survived winter, emerging in spring to build a small nest, around the size of a golf ball, entirely alone. She lays the first batch of eggs herself, and once they hatch into worker wasps, those workers take over building the nest and foraging, while the queen focuses purely on laying more eggs.

Exponential Growth Through Summer

From that point, growth accelerates. More workers mean more nest-building capacity and more food gathered to feed the next batch of larvae, which in turn become more workers. According to the British Pest Control Association, a nest can hold up to 5,000 wasps at its peak, having grown from golf-ball sized in spring to roughly the size of a beach ball by late summer.

Why August Specifically

By August, the colony has usually reached its maximum size for the year, and the nest's priorities shift: rather than just building the nest and feeding larvae, the colony starts producing new queens and males for the following year. Worker wasps, no longer needed for nest expansion, become more visible foraging for sugary food rather than the protein they gathered earlier in the season to feed larvae, which is part of why wasps seem more of a nuisance around food and drink specifically in late summer.

What Happens After Peak

Once new queens have mated and left to find somewhere to overwinter, the rest of the colony, including the original queen, dies off naturally as autumn arrives, and the nest itself is abandoned. It's never reused the following year, though a new queen may choose a similar nearby spot.

Common questions

Is it safe to leave a nest alone until it dies off naturally?

It depends on location. A nest well away from doors, windows or anywhere people pass regularly may be left if it's genuinely not causing a problem, but anywhere near regular foot traffic is worth treating rather than waiting out an entire summer of risk.

Does treating a nest in August work the same as earlier in the season?

Yes, treatment works the same way regardless of colony size, though an August nest holds far more wasps, which is exactly why professional treatment rather than a DIY attempt matters most at this point in the season.
