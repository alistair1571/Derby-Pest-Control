Bed Bugs After a Trip: What to Check First

Bed bugs are one of the easiest pests to bring home without realising, hitching a ride in luggage, clothing or secondhand furniture rather than flying or crawling in on their own. Here's what to check first if you're worried after a trip.

Check Your Luggage Before It Touches the Bedroom

The single most effective habit is checking luggage before bringing it into a bedroom at all. Unpack on a hard floor rather than a carpet or bed, and inspect the seams and lining of suitcases and bags for small reddish-brown insects, about the size of an apple seed, or tiny dark spotting that looks like ground pepper, a sign of droppings.

Wash and Heat-Treat Clothing

Bed bugs and their eggs are killed by heat, so washing clothing on the hottest cycle the fabric allows, then tumble drying on high heat, is one of the most reliable ways to deal with anything that travelled home in your bags. Items that can't be washed can often still be tumble dried on a hot cycle alone for the same effect.

Signs to Watch for Over the Following Days

Bed bugs don't always show themselves immediately. Over the following one to two weeks, watch for clusters of itchy bites in a line, often on arms or legs, appearing on waking. Check mattress seams and bed frame joints for small reddish-brown insects or dark spotting, and be alert to a faint musty smell in the room, a sign of a more established presence.

Why Early Action Matters

Bed bugs don't reflect on how clean a home is, and they can turn up anywhere from a five-star hotel to a family home. What matters is catching it early, since numbers build quickly once bed bugs are established in a mattress seam or bed frame, and an infestation caught in week one is a far smaller job than one caught a month later after it's spread to a second room.

Common questions

Do I need to throw away my luggage?

Usually not. A hot wash or a spell in a sealed bag in direct sun, combined with vacuuming the lining thoroughly, deals with most cases. Heavily infested items are sometimes best discarded, but that's the exception rather than the rule.
