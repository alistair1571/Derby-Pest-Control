Why Are Cockroach Sightings Increasing in the UK?

It's not just anecdotal. Pest controllers across the UK have been reporting a genuine rise in cockroach call outs in recent years, and there's real research behind why.

What the Research Actually Shows

A 2025 review published in the journal Medical and Veterinary Entomology by the Royal Entomological Society looked specifically at how adverse weather events tied to climate change are affecting cockroaches and flies in the UK. Warmer temperatures accelerate how fast cockroaches reproduce, while heavy rainfall and flooding disrupt their usual habitats and push them indoors in search of shelter. High-precipitation events causing sewer overflows are a specific mechanism identified in the research, flushing cockroaches out of drains and forcing them to migrate to new buildings entirely.

It's Not Just the Weather

A British Pest Control Association member support officer, speaking to press about a rise in cases, described the trend as "not unique" to any one city but "something we see across the UK, particularly in busy urban areas with a high turnover of people." That urban, high-turnover pattern matters. Areas with a lot of shared or subdivided housing, and buildings with constant new occupants moving through, such as student housing or short-term lets, give cockroaches more opportunities to be introduced and more places to establish undetected.

Why It Matters More Than It Sounds

Cockroaches carry bacteria including salmonella and E. coli, and for food businesses specifically, an infestation isn't just unpleasant, it's a direct compliance risk under food safety law, with real fines attached for businesses found with an active infestation during an inspection. Catching a rise in activity early, rather than after a colony is fully established, makes a genuine difference to how straightforward treatment is.

Common questions

Is this happening everywhere in the UK, or just certain areas?

The trend is described as widespread rather than isolated to one region, though busy urban areas with high housing turnover tend to see it most.

Does this mean my home or business is more at risk than a few years ago?

Broadly, yes, the underlying conditions driving the increase, warmer temperatures and urban density, aren't going away, which is why noticing early signs and acting on them matters more than it used to.
