What an EHO Actually Checks For

Environmental health officers can turn up at a food business unannounced, during normal trading hours, with no appointment required. Here's what they actually check, and where pest control fits into that.

What Triggers a Visit

EHOs carry out routine, scheduled inspections based on a business's risk rating, but they can also visit in response to a complaint, a reported illness, or simply as part of their normal rounds. There's no requirement to give advance notice, so an inspection can happen on any ordinary trading day.

What They Actually Look At

A typical inspection covers whether the premises and equipment are clean and in good condition, whether pest control measures are in place and evidence of any activity, how food is stored, cooked and dated, whether staff have had food hygiene training, and whether there's a documented food safety management system, commonly a HACCP-based plan, being followed day to day.

Pest control specifically is checked as its own item: an officer will look for signs of activity, droppings, gnaw marks, insect sightings, and ask to see evidence of a pest control contract or recent treatment history if anything raises a concern.

What Powers They Have

EHOs can take photographs, take food samples away for analysis, and review your records, including fridge and freezer temperature logs, cleaning schedules and staff training records. If they find a problem, they can require improvements within a set timeframe and return to re-inspect, or in serious cases issue a notice closing the premises immediately if there's a risk to public health. They can also give evidence in court if a business is prosecuted.

The Food Hygiene Rating

After an inspection, most businesses receive a Food Hygiene Rating from 0 to 5, published on the Food Standards Agency website and often displayed as a sticker in the window. Pest control failures are one of the more common reasons a rating drops, since evidence of activity is treated as a direct hygiene failure rather than a minor note.

Common questions

What's the easiest way to be ready for an unannounced visit?

A documented, scheduled pest control contract with a written report after every visit is the single most useful thing to have ready, since it shows an ongoing history rather than a reactive scramble.

Can an EHO close a business over a pest issue alone?

Yes, if the activity is serious enough to pose an immediate risk to public health, an EHO can issue an emergency notice closing the premises on the spot.
