Mice vs Rats: How to Tell the Difference

Rats and mice cause similar problems but aren't the same job. Getting the identification right before treatment matters, since the entry points, bait placement and follow-up all differ slightly between the two.

Droppings

Rat droppings are larger, roughly the size of a grain of rice or bigger, dark and often tapered at the ends. Mouse droppings are noticeably smaller, more like a grain of black rice, and tend to be scattered more widely rather than clustered, since mice explore more territory relative to their size.

Gnaw Marks and Entry Points

Both gnaw on wood, cables and packaging, but the size of gap each one needs to get in differs sharply. A rat generally needs a gap around the size of a 50 pence piece or a bit larger, while a mouse can squeeze through a gap as small as 6mm, about the width of a pencil. This is the single most useful practical difference: if you can't find an obvious rat-sized gap but there's clear activity, it's more likely mice.

Noise and Movement

Rats are heavier and tend to produce a more noticeable scratching or thumping sound moving through a loft or wall cavity. Mice are lighter and quicker, often producing a faster scurrying or scratching sound, particularly at night, and can be harder to pin down to one exact location since they move around more.

Behaviour and Speed of Infestation

Mice breed faster and in larger numbers than rats, so what looks like a small mouse problem when first noticed can escalate within weeks. Rats are more cautious around new objects, including bait and traps, which is partly why professional placement and patience matter more with rats than with mice.

Why It Matters for Treatment

Both are treated with a similar overall approach, survey, tamper-resistant bait or trapping, and proofing advice, but the details differ. Entry point checks focus on smaller gaps for mice and larger ones for rats, and indoor multi-catch traps are used more often for mice than rats. Getting the identification right on the first visit avoids treating the wrong entry points.

Common questions

Can I have both rats and mice at once?

Yes, it's not unusual, particularly in older properties with multiple entry points. A full survey will identify both if that's what's going on.

Which is worse to have, rats or mice?

Neither is worse exactly, but rats tend to cause more structural damage due to their size, while mice breed faster and can spread through a property more quickly if not caught early.
