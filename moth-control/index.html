"Moths" covers two genuinely different household problems, and it matters which one you've got. The webbing clothes moth is the species behind almost every wardrobe or carpet infestation, causing damage quietly since the adults don't feed and rarely draw attention. Pantry moths are a completely separate issue, turning up in kitchen cupboards rather than bedrooms, and needing a different approach entirely.

This page covers both, how to tell which one you actually have, whether a moth or two even means a real problem, and what a professional visit involves. If you just want it sorted, call the number above, we'll confirm a fixed price on the phone before anything is booked.

Clothes Moths or Pantry Moths? Getting the Right Type

Clothes moths (the webbing clothes moth is the species behind almost every UK case) target natural fibres, wool, silk, fur and feathers, and thrive in the dark, undisturbed conditions of a wardrobe or storage box. They're found on clothing, carpets and upholstery, and synthetic fabrics are largely safe from them.

Pantry moths are an entirely different pest, most commonly the Indian meal moth, found in kitchen cupboards rather than bedrooms. The adult is small with a distinctive two-tone wing pattern, reddish-copper on the outer part of the wing and pale grey nearer the body, and the larvae feed on cereals, flour, nuts, dried fruit and similar stored food, often chewing straight through cardboard or thin plastic packaging to get to it. The first sign is usually fine silk webbing inside a food packet or across the back of a shelf, sometimes with the food itself clumped together where larvae have spun through it.

Is It Really an Infestation, or Just a Stray Moth?

Worth asking before assuming the worst. Plenty of harmless moths wander in through an open window or door on a warm evening, especially over summer, and never establish themselves anywhere in the property. Seeing one or two on their own, with nothing else going on, often isn't a sign of an infestation at all.

What actually points to a genuine problem is repeated sightings in the same spot over time, silk webbing on fabric or inside food packaging, or actual damage, holes in a jumper, or food that's gone clumpy and webbed together in the cupboard. If that's what you're seeing, it's worth getting looked at. If it's just the odd moth passing through, it's usually nothing to worry about.

What Damage Can Moths Do?

Clothes moth larvae spin silk webbing or tubes as they feed and leave behind pepper-like frass, both signs of an established infestation. Damage tends to concentrate in dark, undisturbed spots, under collars and cuffs, in folds of fabric, or beneath furniture where carpet meets skirting board, and it isn't limited to one item, larvae will move between stored clothing, carpets and upholstery within the same room.

Pantry moth larvae don't damage the building or fabrics, but they contaminate food, and an infestation can spread from one packet to neighbouring ones in the same cupboard as larvae crawl between them. Left unchecked, it's not unusual to have to clear and clean an entire cupboard rather than just the one item first noticed.

Signs You Have a Moth Problem

  • Small holes in wool clothing, blankets or carpets
  • Silky webbing or small case-like larval casings in wardrobes or drawers
  • Fine, pepper-like droppings (frass) near affected fabric
  • Silk webbing inside food packaging, or food that's clumped together in a cupboard
  • Small moths flying around the kitchen at night, particularly near stored food
  • Damage concentrated in undisturbed clothing or the back of a rarely checked cupboard

How Do You Get Rid of Moths?

Moth balls and over the counter sprays can deter adult moths but do little against larvae already established in fabric or food packaging, which is where the actual damage happens. Washing individual clothing items helps but won't reach larvae hidden in a carpet edge or under furniture, and throwing out one contaminated food packet doesn't help if others nearby have already been affected too.

A proper approach treats the wider area, wardrobe or cupboard, not just the damaged item, since eggs and larvae are often already present nearby in places that look completely fine.

How We Effectively Remove Moths

For clothes moths, treatment covers wardrobes, drawers and affected carpet areas, since eggs and larvae can be present in items that show no visible damage yet. We'll flag which stored items are most at risk and how to protect them going forward, and guidance on laundering or dry cleaning affected garments is included, since a hot wash or dry clean kills remaining eggs and larvae in items that are otherwise fine to keep.

For pantry moths, we identify the extent of the problem across the cupboard, not just the one packet first noticed, and advise on what needs discarding versus what can be kept once sealed properly. Regular airing and vacuuming of wardrobes, sealed storage for out of season woollens, and sealed containers for dry food are the most effective ways to stop either kind coming back. Common questions

Will treatment damage my clothes?

No, treatment targets the moths and larvae in the space, and we'll advise separately on laundering specific garments if needed.

How do I stop this happening again?

For clothes moths, regular airing and vacuuming of wardrobes, sealed storage for out of season woollens, and treating natural fibre items before long term storage. For pantry moths, sealed containers for dry food and regularly checking the back of cupboards where packets can sit undisturbed for months.

Is it the same as carpet beetle treatment?

A similar principle but a different pest and slightly different treatment approach. We'll confirm which it is on inspection, since the signs can look alike.

I've only seen one or two moths, do I actually have an infestation?

Not necessarily. Plenty of harmless moths wander in through an open window or door on a summer evening and never establish themselves. What points to a genuine infestation is repeated sightings in the same spot, silk webbing on fabric or in food packaging, or actual damage, holes in clothing or clumped, webbed-together food. If you're only seeing the odd moth with no other signs, it's often nothing to treat.

What's the difference between clothes moths and pantry moths?

Clothes moths target natural fibres, wool, silk, fur, and are found in wardrobes, carpets and stored fabrics. Pantry moths target dried food, cereals, flour, nuts, dried fruit, and are found in kitchen cupboards. They look different, the most common pantry moth has a distinctive two-tone reddish-copper and grey wing pattern, and they need different treatment, so it's worth telling us which one you're dealing with when you call.
